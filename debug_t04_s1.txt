ACB — Construction and Working
ACB (Air Circuit Breaker) is the main switching and protection device in the LV switchboard. Rated for full load current and short circuit breaking.

CONSTRUCTION COMPONENTS:
   1. MAIN CONTACTS: Silver-tungsten alloy — high conductivity, arc-resistant. Carry full load current normally.
   2. ARC CONTACTS (arcing contacts): Carbon or copper-graphite — make FIRST and break LAST. Take the arc — main contacts are protected from arc damage.
   3. ARC CHUTES: Insulating ceramic or fibre plates (arc splitter plates) above the contacts. Arc drawn into chutes, split into multiple shorter arcs, cooled and extinguished.
   4. OPERATING MECHANISM: Spring-charged mechanism — stores energy — releases on trip or close command. Both closing spring and opening spring.
   5. TRIP COIL (TC): Electromagnet — when energised — releases latch — opens contacts instantly.
   6. CLOSE COIL (CC): Electromagnet — closes contacts against spring.
   7. SHUNT TRIP: External trip signal (from protection relays) energises trip coil.
   8. UNDERVOLTAGE TRIP (UVT / NVC): Opens ACB when control voltage is lost — fail-safe.

HOW ARC IS EXTINGUISHED IN ACB:
   Step 1: Trip signal received — operating mechanism releases — contacts START to open.
   Step 2: Main contacts open first. ARC forms between contacts.
   Step 3: Arc is DRIVEN by magnetic force (from arc current) into the arc chutes.
   Step 4: In arc chutes: arc is split into many shorter series arcs by splitter plates.
   Step 5: Each shorter arc has higher voltage drop — total arc voltage exceeds supply voltage.
   Step 6: Arc cannot be sustained — extinguishes. Circuit is broken.
   Step 7: Arc chute material absorbs heat. Ionised gases cool and de-ionise.

ACB RATINGS:
   In (Normal current rating): continuous current the ACB can carry.
   Ics / Icu (Short circuit breaking capacity): maximum current ACB can interrupt safely.
   Ue (Rated operational voltage): maximum voltage for operation.
   Insulation voltage Ui: maximum voltage for insulation (higher than Ue).
