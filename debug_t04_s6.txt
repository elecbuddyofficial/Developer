Bus Tie Breaker — Purpose and Operation
[TABLE]
BUS TIE BREAKER — Exam Answer
The bus tie breaker (BTB) connects two separate MSB sections (Section 1 and Section 2). It allows power to flow between sections.

NORMAL OPERATION — OPEN BUS TIE:
   Each MSB section has its own generator(s). BTB is OPEN. Sections operate independently.
   Advantage: fault on one section does not affect other section.
   Higher supply security — section fault is isolated.

CLOSED BUS TIE — When Used:
   Both sections connected together — all generators supply common load.
   Used when: one generator on each section cannot handle its section load alone, or when generators being transferred between sections.

SECTIONALISED OPERATION (normal for large ships):
   Port section: Generator 1 + Generator 2 (or 3) feeding port side loads.
   Starboard section: Generator 3 + Generator 4 feeding starboard loads.
   BTB open: maximum fault isolation. Any busbar fault only affects one section.

INTERLOCK WITH BTB:
   Some ships: closing BTB automatically opens one generator ACB — prevents parallel overloading.
   Others: BTB interlocked — cannot close unless generators on both sections are paralleled first.

EMERGENCY USE:
   If generator on one section fails: close BTB — power restored to that section from other section's generator.
   ETOs must know where BTB panel is and how to close it in emergency.
