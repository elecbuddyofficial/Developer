What is a UPS and how does online UPS work? | UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) provides continuous AC power to critical loads with zero interruption on mains failure. Online (double conversion) UPS: AC mains → rectifier → DC bus (batteries connected in parallel) → inverter → AC output. Load always fed through inverter — never from mains directly. Mains fail: batteries maintain DC bus instantly — inverter continues — zero switchover time. Output is perfectly regulated and isolated from mains disturbances. Used for: ECDIS, GMDSS, IAS, fire alarm, navigation systems.
What is the bypass switch on a UPS? | Bypass switch routes load directly from mains to load — bypassing UPS rectifier, inverter, and batteries. Static bypass (automatic): thyristor switch — instantly transfers to mains bypass if UPS faults or overloads — prevents load loss. Maintenance bypass (manual): isolates entire UPS for maintenance while load continues from mains — no interruption. Both are essential: static bypass provides automatic protection, maintenance bypass allows full UPS shutdown without interrupting critical systems.

SHORE CONNECTION — Complete Exam Answer
Shore connection (cold ironing) provides shore power to the ship in port — allowing ship's generators to be shut down, reducing emissions and noise in port.

SOLAS REGULATION: SOLAS II-1 Reg 4(1) — ships must be capable of connecting to shore power if shore supply is available.
IEC 80005-1: International standard for high voltage shore connections (HVSC).

SHORE CONNECTION VOLTAGE MISMATCH PROBLEM:
   Shore supply may be: 440V, 400V, 6.6kV, 11kV at 50Hz or 60Hz.
   Ship's system: 440V or 6.6kV at 50Hz (or 60Hz).
   Mismatch in voltage or frequency must be handled.

ISOLATION TRANSFORMER — WHY ESSENTIAL:
   Shore supply is SOLIDLY EARTHED (TN-S system) — neutral connected to ground.
   Ship uses UNEARTHED (IT) system — neutral floating or NER.
   Without isolation transformer: connecting shore supply directly would EARTH the ship's system through shore earth → stray currents → corrosion → galvanic cell with seawater → hull corrosion.
   Isolation transformer breaks the earth path — shore earth does NOT connect to ship's system.
   Also: transforms voltage if shore voltage ≠ ship voltage.
   Also: prevents shore system faults from propagating to ship system.

FREQUENCY CONVERTER:
   If shore is 60Hz and ship is 50Hz: frequency converter (or VFD-based converter) needed.
   Some ports use static frequency converters — rectifier → DC link → inverter at correct frequency.

SHORE CONNECTION PROCEDURE:
   Step 1: Verify shore supply voltage, frequency, and phase sequence.
   Step 2: Connect shore cable to ship's shore connection box.
   Step 3: Verify isolation transformer output matches ship's bus voltage.
   Step 4: Synchronise shore supply with running ship's generators (if transferring under load).
   Step 5: Close shore connection ACB — transfer load.
   Step 6: Open ship's generator ACB — stop generators.

EXAMINER QUESTION | IDEAL ANSWER
Why is an isolation transformer needed for shore connection? | Shore supply is solidly earthed (TN-S system — neutral connected to ground). Ship uses unearthed IT system (floating neutral or NER). Direct connection without isolation transformer: shore earth connects to ship system → creates earth path → stray currents through seawater → electrolytic corrosion of hull and underwater fittings. Isolation transformer breaks the galvanic path — shore earth does NOT connect to ship's neutral. Also provides voltage transformation if shore voltage differs from ship's voltage, and prevents shore faults from entering ship's system.
What is cold ironing? | Cold ironing (Alternative Maritime Power — AMP) is connecting ship to shore electrical supply in port, allowing ship's generators to be shut down. Benefits: eliminates exhaust emissions from generators while in port (SOx, NOx, particulates), reduces noise, reduces fuel consumption. Required by some ports (California, major European ports) for environmental regulations. Requires: shore connection box on ship, isolation transformer (mandatory), frequency converter if frequency mismatch, sufficient shore supply capacity. IEC 80005-1 standard covers HVSC (High Voltage Shore Connection) systems.

PREFERENTIAL TRIP — Complete Exam Answer
PREFERENTIAL TRIP (load shedding relay): automatically disconnects non-essential loads in stages when generator is overloaded — prevents complete blackout.

WHY PREFERENTIAL TRIP?
   When generator is overloaded: overcurrent relay would trip the generator → complete blackout.
   Better to lose non-essential loads than lose ALL power.
   Preferential trip sheds loads in priority order — keeps essential services alive.

PRIORITY ORDER — WHAT IS NEVER SHED:
   NEVER SHED: Navigation (GMDSS, radar, ECDIS), Steering gear, Fire pump, Fire detection, Emergency bilge, CCTV, IAS.

LOAD SHEDDING STAGES (typical):
   STAGE 1 (load reaches 90% of generator rating):
   Shed: galley ovens, water heaters, laundry, non-essential HVAC, workshop equipment.
   Time delay: 5-10 seconds (dashpot or electronic timer).

   STAGE 2 (load still high after Stage 1 — typically >95%):
   Shed: remaining HVAC, accommodation loads, deck equipment.
   Time delay: additional 5-10 seconds.

   STAGE 3 (load still exceeds generator capacity):
   OCR trips generator — unavoidable — blackout despite preferential trip.

HOW PREFERENTIAL TRIP DETECTS OVERLOAD:
   Current transformer (CT) on generator output — feeds overload sensor.
   When current exceeds first threshold: Stage 1 shed relay energises — time delay begins.
   If current reduces: timer resets — no further shedding.
   If current stays high after delay: shed relay operates — opens non-essential feeders.

DASHPOT — TIME DELAY DEVICE (surveyor asks this):
   Pneumatic/hydraulic piston and cylinder with needle valve orifice.
   Relay energises → piston moves slowly (restricted by orifice) → time delay before contacts operate.
   Adjust needle valve: more open = faster = shorter delay. More closed = longer delay.
   Modern replacement: electronic timer relay — more reliable, digitally adjustable.

EXAMINER QUESTION | IDEAL ANSWER
What is preferential trip and how does it work? | Preferential trip (load shedding relay) sheds non-essential loads in stages when generator is overloaded — prevents complete blackout. CT monitors generator current. When current exceeds Stage 1 threshold (90% rating): after dashpot/electronic delay (5-10 sec): galley, HVAC, non-essential equipment shed. If still high: Stage 2 sheds more loads (additional delay). Essential services NEVER shed: navigation, steering, fire pump, fire detection, GMDSS, bilge. Preferential trip prevents OCR from tripping generator — losing all power is worse than losing non-essential loads.
What loads are never shed by preferential trip? | Essential safety loads permanently protected — never shed by preferential trip: navigation equipment (GMDSS, radar, ECDIS, gyro, AIS), steering gear motor supply, fire detection and alarm system, fire pump, emergency bilge pump, emergency lighting supply, internal communications, navigation lights, SSAS. These are hardwired to essential bus or directly to MSB with overload protection only — not connected through preferential trip switchgear. Loss of any of these would create immediate SOLAS violation and safety hazard.

FINAL MEMORY AID — Switchboard & Circuit Breakers
ACB: Arc chutes split arc → shorter arcs → arc voltage > supply → extinguishes. Arc contacts protect main contacts.
REVERSE POWER: Prime mover fails → generator motors → reverse power relay trips ACB. 5-10% rated, 3-5 sec delay.
UNDER-FREQ: Generator overloaded → slows → frequency drops → motors slow → transformers overheat. Stage alarms then trips.
EMERGENCY GEN: 45 sec (cargo), 30 sec (passenger). Above bulkhead deck. Outside ER. 18hr fuel. 2 starts. Monthly test.
SEQUENTIAL RESTORE: Cooling → LO → fuel → boiler → air → HVAC → galley. 30 sec-2 min delays. PMS automates this.
BATTERIES: Lead-acid 2V/cell SG test. Ni-Cd 1.2V/cell robust. GMDSS battery: 6hr (no UPS) or 1hr (with UPS). Replace at 80%.
UPS ONLINE: Always through rectifier → DC bus → inverter. Zero switchover. Static bypass = auto fault transfer. Maintenance bypass = isolate UPS.
SHORE CONNECTION: Isolation transformer MANDATORY — prevents shore earth connecting to ship IT system — stops hull corrosion.
PREFERENTIAL TRIP: 90% load → shed galley/HVAC (delay). 95% → shed more. NEVER shed: nav, steering, fire pump, GMDSS.
GROUP STARTER PANEL: MCC — multiple starters centralised. GREEN=stopped, RED=running, AMBER=fault, WHITE=power.
