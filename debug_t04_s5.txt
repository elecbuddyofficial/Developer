Under-Frequency Protection

Under-Frequency Protection
[TABLE]
UNDER-FREQUENCY RELAY — Exam Answer
PURPOSE: Trips non-essential loads (or generator ACB as last resort) when system frequency drops below set value.

WHY FREQUENCY DROPS:
   Generator overloaded — prime mover cannot provide enough power — slows down — frequency drops.
   Sudden large load added (bow thruster start, large motor start) without adequate generation.
   Generator trip — remaining generators overloaded — frequency drops.

WHAT HAPPENS AT LOW FREQUENCY:
   All motors slow down proportionally (synchronous speed ∝ frequency).
   Cooling pumps, lubrication pumps slow — overheating risk.
   Transformers: flux increases (V/f ratio increases) — iron core saturation — overheating.
   Navigation, GMDSS, control systems may malfunction.

UNDER-FREQUENCY RELAY ACTION:
   Stage 1 (e.g., below 48Hz): alarm — operator alerted.
   Stage 2 (e.g., below 47Hz): preferential trip — non-essential loads shed.
   Stage 3 (e.g., below 45Hz): generator ACB trips — prevents generator damage.
   (SOLAS: frequency should recover to within +/-5% in 5 seconds after disturbance.)

SETTING: Typically 47-48Hz alarm, 45-46Hz trip for 50Hz system.
[/TABLE]

EXAMINER QUESTION | IDEAL ANSWER
What is the reverse power relay and how is it tested? | Reverse power relay trips generator ACB when generator absorbs power from busbar (prime mover failed — generator being motored). Without it: engine mechanically damaged (runs without combustion). Setting: 5-10% rated kW reverse, 3-5 second delay. Testing: METHOD 1 — inject simulated reverse power signal into relay with ACB in test position using power relay test set. METHOD 2 — during shore power transfer, observe relay operation. Verify relay operates at set value and ACB trips.
Why is under-frequency protection important? | Frequency drops when generator overloaded — prime mover slows. Effects of low frequency: all motors slow (synchronous speed proportional to frequency) — pumps and fans reduce flow — overheating. Transformers overheat (V/f increases — iron saturation). Navigation and control systems malfunction. Under-frequency relay: Stage 1 (48Hz) — alarm. Stage 2 (47Hz) — preferential trip sheds non-essential loads. Stage 3 (45Hz) — generator ACB trips. Prevents cascading failure. SOLAS requires frequency recovery within +/-5% in 5 seconds.
How do you test an overcurrent relay by secondary injection? | 1. Rack ACB to TEST position — power contacts isolated. 2. Disconnect CT secondary leads from relay. 3. Connect secondary injection test set to relay current input. 4. Inject test current at multiples of set value (1.05x, 2x, 5x, 10x set current). 5. Record trip time at each multiple. 6. Compare with IDMT curve from relay manual — verify within tolerance. 7. Check relay trip output operates ACB trip coil. 8. Reconnect CT secondary — rack ACB to service. 9. Record all results in maintenance log with test set calibration details.
